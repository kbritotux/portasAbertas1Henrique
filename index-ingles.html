Virtual reality (VR) lets users interact with digital environments in an immersive way. VR headsets combine several features to create this experience, including 3D visuals, motion tracking, audio, and vibration. Here are three key aspects of how the technology works.

  • 3D Vision

    Headsets show each eye a slightly different image, creating a sense of depth and a three-dimensional view.

  • Interaction and Movement

    Sensors track head and controller movements, enabling more natural interaction with the virtual environment.

  • Immersive Features

    Eye tracking, 3D audio, and vibration can strengthen the feeling of being inside a virtual environment.

Headset

Device responsible for displaying the virtual environment and providing visual immersion.

Controllers

Allow the user to interact with objects and elements within the virtual environment.

Sensors

Detect movements of the head, hands, and body to track the user in the virtual environment.

Audio and Vibration

Sounds and haptic feedback help increase the sense of presence and immersion.

Areas of Use

Entertainment and Gaming

VR lets users enter and interact with digital environments, making games and other entertainment experiences more immersive.

Films and Audiovisual Experiences

Headsets offer a different way to enjoy content, creating virtual environments that make viewers feel part of the experience.

Education

Schools and institutions can use VR to create interactive lessons and experiences, letting learners explore places, historical events, and concepts visually.

Work and Training

Businesses can use VR for training, simulations, and virtual meetings, allowing people to practice tasks in controlled environments.

Healthcare and Medicine

VR can support professional training through simulations and can also be used in some rehabilitation and therapy applications.

Communication and Social Interaction

Virtual environments let people in different places meet, talk, and take part in activities through avatars, creating new forms of digital interaction.

The First Steps:

Charles Wheatstone developed stereoscopic viewers that created a sense of depth by combining two images. Decades later, in 1935, writer Stanley Weinbaum imagined goggles that could deliver experiences with images, sounds, and smells—a concept remarkably close to modern VR.

The Technology Emerges:

Morton Heilig created the Sensorama, a device that combined images, sound, motion, and aromas for an immersive experience. In 1968, Ivan Sutherland and Bob Sproull developed the Sword of Damocles, considered one of the first functional VR headsets.

Growing Popularity and Challenges:

VPL Research helped establish the term “virtual reality” and developed devices such as digital gloves and goggles. In the 1990s, VR reached video games, but high prices and limitations in comfort and quality caused public interest to decline.

The VR Revival:

In 2012, the Oculus Rift gained attention through a Kickstarter campaign, helping bring virtual reality back into the spotlight. Companies such as Sony, HTC, Google, and Samsung then invested in the technology, which is now used in gaming, education, science, training, and simulation.

Pioneers

Morton Heilig

He developed the Sensorama in the 1950s and 1960s, a machine combining 3D images, sound, motion, wind, and scents for an immersive experience. He is considered a VR pioneer.

Ivan Sutherland

In 1968, he and his student Bob Sproull created the Sword of Damocles, one of the first computer-connected, head-mounted virtual reality systems.

Jaron Lanier

He helped popularize virtual reality in the 1980s. He founded VPL Research, which developed devices such as the DataGlove and EyePhone. He is often credited with coining or popularizing the term “virtual reality.”

Thomas Furness

An engineer who spent decades developing visual display systems and flight simulators, he is often called one of the “grandfathers” of virtual reality and strongly influenced immersive interfaces.
